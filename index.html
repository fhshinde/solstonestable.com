Six things we fix all the time

These are typical examples of the kind of changes we set up — every business is different, and we'll tell you straight which ones apply to yours.

Inside the business

Rosters, timesheets and payroll.

Before: the whiteboard, paper timesheets, and Sunday nights doing payroll maths.

After: staff clock in on their phones, the system tallies the hours and rates itself, and payroll is ready in minutes — checked by you, not calculated by you.

Running out of stock.

Before: reordering by eye and finding the gap when a customer asks.

After: the software learns your sales patterns, flags what's running low before it runs out, and drafts the supplier order for you to approve with one tap.

Knowing your numbers.

Before: finding out how the month went at tax time.

After: a Monday-morning snapshot on your phone — sales, cash, who owes you what — with a short plain-English summary, written by AI, of what changed last week and why.

For your customers

Enquiries at all hours.

Before: calls missed while you're with a customer, messages answered days later — or never.

After: an AI assistant replies in seconds, any hour — prices, opening times, availability — takes booking details, and hands the tricky questions to you. Your customers get an answer; you get your hands back.

No-shows.

Before: booked appointments quietly not turning up, and the slot wasted.

After: automatic reminders the day before with one-tap confirm or rebook. The system does the chasing so you never have to.

Customers who never come back.

Before: a great first visit, then silence — you're too busy to follow up.

After: the system remembers every customer and sends the right message at the right moment — a thank-you, a service-due reminder, a request for a review — so regulars actually become regulars.

What owners are usually after — and what we design for: hours back every week, customers who return, staff who need less chasing, and numbers you can actually see. We won't promise figures before we've looked at your business. Anyone who does is guessing.
