Six things we fix all the time

These are typical examples of the kind of changes we set up — every business is different, and we'll tell you straight which ones apply to yours.

A website that looks its age.

Before: a site built years ago that's hard to read on a phone — quietly costing you the customers who looked you up before deciding.

After: a clean, fast site that works properly on a phone and looks like a business worth trusting — so younger customers take you seriously from the first click.

Still waiting to get paid.

Before: cash and bank transfers, invoices settled whenever the customer gets round to it, and you doing the chasing.

After: customers pay by card, PayNow or a QR code on the invoice, and reminders go out on their own. Money arrives sooner and reconciles itself.

The same details, typed three times.

Before: an order written on a pad, keyed into the system, then keyed again into the accounts — with a typo somewhere in the middle.

After: entered once, and it flows everywhere it's needed. Fewer mistakes, and hours a week nobody has to spend re-typing.

Decisions made on gut feel.

Before: you know it's been busy, but not which days earn their keep, which jobs actually make money, or which quietly lose it.

After: a plain-English summary on your phone each week — what changed and why — so the next decision rests on what happened, not what you remember.

Ordering by eye.

Before: reordering from memory, then finding the gap when a customer asks for the one thing you've run out of.

After: the system learns what sells and when, flags what's running low before it runs out, and drafts the supplier order for you to approve.

Everything important is in your head.

Before: only you know the supplier prices, the regulars' preferences and how the tricky jobs get done — so little moves while you're away.

After: the way you work written down and built into the system, so your team can follow it and the place runs steadily without you standing over it.
